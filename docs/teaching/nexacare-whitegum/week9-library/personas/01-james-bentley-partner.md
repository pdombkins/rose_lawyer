# Persona — James Bentley, Relationship Partner (NexaCare)

**Role:** Partner. Owns the NexaCare relationship; has acted for their General
Counsel personally since before she moved in-house. Charge-out $850/hr.

**Part in the change:** Sponsor by default. Nothing on this matter happens
against his stated preference, and nothing happens quickly without his
visible support.

## How he works today

Reconstructs time in blocks at the end of the week, or at the end of the
month, from his calendar and sent items. Considers this normal and does not
regard it as a problem — "the number comes out right".

Runs the client relationship through direct calls. Matter updates go out when
he happens to speak to the GC, which is often, so from where he sits the
client is well served. Task status in the practice-management system is
something the team maintains; he has opened it three times.

Delegates freely and trusts his team. Reviews drafts closely, process not at
all.

## What he says

> "The client has never once complained about how we communicate. Show me the
> complaint."

> "I'm not going to have my associates filling in forms while a deal is live.
> We can tidy the record afterwards."

> "If it makes us faster, fine. But I'm not signing up to something that makes
> me look like I'm being audited by my own firm."

## What matters for the assessment

His resistance, if it comes, will not be to the tool. It will be to
visibility — a contemporaneous record makes his own working patterns legible
to the firm in a way they currently are not, and he is the person with the
most autonomy to lose.

He is also the single highest-value convert available: a partner saying this
worked carries more weight with the other partners than any amount of
business-services advocacy.

**Check before you assess him.** Look at what the ledger says about how his
NexaCare time was recorded, and how much of it there is against what was
planned. Do not take his account of his own practice at face value, and do not
take the data at face value either — ask what a low figure could mean other
than the obvious.
