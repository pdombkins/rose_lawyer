# Persona — Aisha Rahman, Junior Associate

**Role:** Junior associate, second year. Charge-out $300/hr. Carries a large
share of the NexaCare task plan by estimated hours.

**Part in the change:** Does what she is asked. That makes her easy to
mandate and easy to overlook — and she is where an unworkable process will
first show up as a quiet workaround.

## How she works today

Records time daily because she was told to in her first week and has not
stopped. Her entries are the most complete in the firm, and she assumes
everyone else's are too.

Learns the system by trial and error. Has never been shown the task plan for
the matter she is working on, so she experiences the matter as a sequence of
instructions rather than as a plan with a shape.

Will not say she is overloaded. Will show it in her recorded hours if anyone
looks.

## What she says

> "Nobody's ever told me what the plan is. I get the next thing when the
> previous thing is done."

> "I'd rather be told the standard than guess it and be corrected in a
> review."

> "If it's what we do now, I'll do it. Is it what we do now?"

## What matters for the assessment

Her adoption is cheap, and that makes her tempting to count as a win. Resist
that. Her adoption proves nothing to anyone who matters, because nobody in the
partnership is watching what the juniors do.

Her real value in the change is diagnostic: she will comply with a broken
process rather than object to it, so if the design is wrong, the evidence will
appear in her rework and her hours rather than in her feedback. Build a way to
notice that.

Also note the cost asymmetry — an hour of her time is a third of a senior
associate's charge-out, so shifting effort towards her looks efficient on a
fee model and can be a false economy on a rework model.

**Check before you assess her.** Look at her recorded time against her
assigned estimates, and against the same ratio for the others on the matter.
