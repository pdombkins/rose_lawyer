# Kendry & Slate — values, service promise and EVP

Internal artefacts, last reviewed 2019. Teaching material for LAWS3850 Week 8.

*Use these with the Week-8 EX and CX workflows. The exercise is to compare what
is stated here against what the matter record actually shows.*

## Our values

**Excellence.** We deliver the highest quality legal work, without exception.

**Partnership.** We are trusted advisers, not suppliers. We think like our
clients think.

**Integrity.** We do the right thing, always, even when it is commercially
inconvenient.

**One firm.** We work as a single team across offices and practice groups.

**People.** Our people are our greatest asset.

## Our service promise

*Exceptional legal minds, commercially applied. No surprises.*

## Employee value proposition

Kendry & Slate offers ambitious lawyers the opportunity to work on Australia's
most complex transactions alongside recognised market leaders. We invest in our
people through structured development, genuine mentoring from senior
practitioners, and a culture where merit is recognised and rewarded.

We are committed to sustainable working practices and to supporting the
wellbeing of every member of our team.

## What the firm measures and rewards

Extract from the current partnership remuneration framework and associate
review criteria.

| Measure | Weighting |
|---|---|
| Billable hours against target (1,750 for associates) | 40% |
| Realisation rate (billed vs recorded) | 20% |
| Origination and client relationships | 25% |
| Contribution to firm (BD, training, recruitment) | 10% |
| Client feedback | 5% |

Associate promotion is assessed annually. Client feedback is collected at
matter close where the responsible partner elects to request it.

There is no measure for supervision quality, for accuracy of estimates, or for
whether a matter was staffed sustainably.

## Notes for the exercise

Green's argument is that culture is the collective mindset employees actually
bring to work, which is shaped by what an organisation measures and rewards
rather than by what it states. Read the values and the service promise against
the measurement table, and then against the matter record.

Specific questions:

1. The service promise says "no surprises". Which measure in the table creates
   pressure toward surprises?
2. The values say people are the greatest asset. What proportion of the
   assessment framework is about how people are treated or developed?
3. Realisation rate is 20% of the assessment. What behaviour does that reward
   when a matter runs over estimate, and which Week-8 challenge scenario does
   that map onto?
4. Client feedback is 5%, collected at the responsible partner's election.
   What does that produce in the feedback data, and who is systematically
   missing from it?
