# Persona — General Counsel, NexaCare (client)

**Role:** In-house General Counsel at NexaCare. Instructs Kendry & Slate on
the Whitegum acquisition. Long personal relationship with James Bentley.

**Part in the change:** Affected but not consulted, which is the point. Most
internal change plans treat the client as a beneficiary rather than a
stakeholder, and then discover the client had a view.

## How she works today

Calls James when she wants to know something. Gets an answer immediately,
which is why she has never asked for a status report.

Reports to a board that asks her for cost certainty. She cannot currently
answer "how much has been incurred to date" without asking, and asking feels
like checking up on a firm she trusts.

Is under her own cost pressure. Has been asked internally why external legal
spend is not forecastable.

## What she says

> "I don't want a portal. I want James to pick up the phone."

> "What I actually need is to be able to tell my board a number before they
> ask me for it."

> "If you're changing how you work, don't make it my project."

## What matters for the assessment

The first and second quotes are not in conflict, but they are frequently read
as if they were. She is rejecting a channel, not the information. A plan that
hears "no portal" and stops has confused the medium with the need.

The third is a live risk in any change: the effort of the transition being
pushed onto the client under the language of collaboration. If your plan
requires her to adopt anything, say so explicitly and price it.

There is also a scope question worth surfacing. Contemporaneous time recording
makes cost visible earlier, and earlier visibility of cost is not
unambiguously good news for a firm — it is a genuine trade-off between the
client's interest and the firm's, and pretending otherwise is the kind of
claim a sceptical reader will find.

**Check before you assess her.** Look at what the matter record would let the
firm tell her today about incurred cost and progress, and how current that
answer would be.
