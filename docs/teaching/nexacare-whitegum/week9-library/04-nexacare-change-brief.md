# The Week-9 change brief — implementing the NexaCare happy path

The scenario for this week's group exercise. Read it before you start; it is
what your change plan is a plan *for*.

## The proposal

In Week 8 your group designed the client "happy path" for the NexaCare
engagement — the sequence of touchpoints, from first conference through to
after-matter review, that the client should experience.

Kendry & Slate's managing partner has now agreed to implement it on the
NexaCare/Whitegum matter as a pilot, with a view to rolling it out across the
firm's corporate matters if it works.

Implementation requires four behavioural changes from the matter team:

1. **Contemporaneous time recording** — time entered on the day it is worked,
   rather than reconstructed later.
2. **Task status maintained in the system** — the matter plan is the single
   source of truth for progress, replacing personal spreadsheets and verbal
   chasing.
3. **Defined client touchpoints** — scheduled matter updates at agreed
   intervals, in addition to whatever ad hoc contact happens.
4. **A visible matter plan** — everyone on the matter, including the
   paralegal and the juniors, can see the full plan and their part in it.

No new software is required. Every one of these is possible in the system
today.

## The constraints you are working within

- **90 days.** The pilot must show something by the end of the quarter,
  because that is when the partnership next reviews it.
- **No change to billable targets** — unless your plan makes the case for one
  and identifies who has the authority to grant it. Priya Iyer sits on the
  remuneration committee.
- **The client cannot be given work.** Anything requiring NexaCare to adopt
  something must be identified and justified separately.
- **The matter is live.** This is not a greenfield rollout; the acquisition
  timetable does not pause for the change programme.

## What you are asked to produce

A change management approach that would give this pilot a genuine chance of
working. The Week-9 workflows in Rose take you through it in sequence:

| | Workflow | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Change urgency — the evidence | The case for change, from the data |
| 2 | Guiding coalition & diffusion map | Who leads and who you start with |
| 3 | Change vision on a page | The five-minute version |
| 4 | Communicate the vision — audience pack | Message, messenger, channel, cadence |
| 5 | Change impact assessment (tabular) | Per-persona impact grid |
| 6 | Resistance & loss map | What people lose, and your response |
| 7 | Expectancy Theory motivation design | Why anyone would actually do it |
| 8 | Quick wins — 90 day plan | Three engineered wins with baselines |
| 9 | Kotter readiness review | Your own plan, hard-marked |
| 10 | Week 9 change pack | The assembled, exported deliverable |

The seven personas in the Library are the rows for workflow 5. Each is a
separate document so the tabular review can treat them as separate subjects.

## The question the whole week turns on

Every one of the four changes above is individually trivial. Together they
change what a lawyer's Tuesday looks like, they make individual working
patterns visible to the firm for the first time, and they cost time that is
not billable.

So: **why would James Bentley do this?**

Not why should he. Why would he. A plan that can only answer the first
question is the plan Kotter is writing about.
