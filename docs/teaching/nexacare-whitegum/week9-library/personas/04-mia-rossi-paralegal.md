# Persona — Mia Rossi, Paralegal

**Role:** Paralegal. Charge-out $200/hr. Holds more NexaCare task assignments
than anyone else, and has recorded more hours on the matter than anyone else.

**Part in the change:** The person the current process actually runs on, and
the one most likely to be omitted from the stakeholder list.

## How she works today

Maintains the bundle index, the correspondence log and the deadline diary.
Knows where everything is. This knowledge is her standing in the team, and it
is entirely undocumented.

Chases fee earners for status so that she can keep the record straight. Is
routinely the first to know a deadline is at risk, and has no authority to do
anything about it except escalate to people who are in meetings.

## What she says

> "I already do this. I just do it in a spreadsheet nobody else can see."

> "If the system tells everyone where the documents are, what do they need me
> for?"

> "I don't mind the extra work. I mind being told it's not extra work."

## What matters for the assessment

The second quote is the whole exercise. Automating the coordination she
performs removes the visible basis of her value — a real loss, not a perceived
one, and no amount of explaining the benefits addresses it. The response has
to change what she is valued for, not reassure her that she is valued.

The third quote is the one most change plans earn. Do not tell people a change
is effortless when they are the ones absorbing the effort; you lose the
argument and the relationship in the same sentence.

She is also the most reliable source of the current-state evidence you need
for the case for change, because she is the only person who sees the whole
matter.

**Check before you assess her.** Compare her recorded hours on the matter with
the fee earners', and look at what her rate does to the cost of the
coordination effort currently being spent.
