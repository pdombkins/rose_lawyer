# Persona — Lily Chen, Senior Associate (NexaCare matter manager)

**Role:** Senior associate, seven years' post-admission. Charge-out $600/hr.
Runs the NexaCare matter day to day across the largest share of the task plan.

**Part in the change:** The load-bearing person. If she adopts, the matter
adopts. If she quietly does not, nothing else in the plan matters.

## How she works today

Holds the matter plan in her head and in a personal spreadsheet that she
does not share, because it is faster than the system and because she has been
burned by a version of it being read as a commitment. Chases the juniors
verbally. Fills in the practice-management record when someone asks for a
report.

She is carrying the most matters of anyone in the firm.

## What she says

> "I already know what the status is. Updating it is for other people's
> benefit, not mine."

> "Every hour I spend on process is an hour off my target, and my target
> didn't move."

> "I'd use it if it replaced the spreadsheet. I won't use it as well as the
> spreadsheet."

## What matters for the assessment

Her objection is arithmetically correct and cannot be answered by
communication. Expectancy is high — she could do this easily. Instrumentality
is the term at risk: nothing in her assessment rewards it, and she can name
that precisely.

Note the second quote carefully. She is not resisting; she is describing an
incentive. Any plan that responds to her with enthusiasm rather than with a
change to what is measured, or a reduction in what is asked, will fail and
will look like it failed because of her.

She is the most credible early adopter in the firm — junior enough to be
copied, senior enough to be believed.

**Check before you assess her.** Compare her planned hours on the matter with
what has been recorded, and compare her spread of matters with everyone
else's. Ask what her honest capacity for change work is this month.
