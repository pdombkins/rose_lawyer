# Persona — Priya Iyer, Partner (not on NexaCare)

**Role:** Partner. Runs her own matters and her own team. Charge-out $850/hr.
Has no involvement in NexaCare and no obligation to care about it.

**Part in the change:** The audience. NexaCare is the pilot; she is who the
pilot is *for*. A change that works on one matter and does not travel to her
practice is a project, not a transformation.

## How she works today

Her matters run well by her own account and by the client's. She has her own
way of doing things, developed over fifteen years, and she is measurably
successful with it.

She sits on the remuneration committee.

## What she says

> "Great. Show me it worked, on numbers I recognise, and I'll think about it."

> "Every few years someone rolls out a system. I've stopped learning them
> until I see who's still using it in a year."

> "You want my team's time for this. What comes off their targets?"

## What matters for the assessment

She is the early majority in Rogers' terms, and she is behaving exactly as the
model predicts: she will not move on a launch, and she will move on evidence
from a peer. Note that she is asking for evidence, not refusing — treating her
as a blocker is the standard error.

Her third question is the one most plans cannot answer, and she is on the
committee that could answer it. That makes her simultaneously the hardest
person to convince and the only one who can remove the structural barrier.
Recruiting her is therefore worth more than recruiting three enthusiasts.

Her second quote is a precise description of Kotter's error 7. She has watched
victory be declared before, and she is pricing that history into her response.

**Check before you assess her.** Look at whether the practice-management data
would let you make a like-for-like comparison between her matters and
NexaCare. If it would not, that is a finding about the measurement plan, not
about her.
