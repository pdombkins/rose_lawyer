# Week 9 — Transformation and Change Management

**One hour. Five activities. Work in your group.**

Everything you need is in the Library folder **Week 9 — Change management**.

## The scenario, in short

Your Week-8 client happy path has been approved as a **90-day pilot on the
NexaCare matter**. Four behavioural asks of the team: record time
contemporaneously, keep task status in the system, hold defined client
touchpoints, and make the matter plan visible. No new software — everything is
already possible in K&S today.

Every one of those is individually trivial. Together they change what a
lawyer's Tuesday looks like, they make individual working patterns visible for
the first time, and they cost time that is not billable.

## The five activities

### 1. Change urgency — the evidence · ~12 min

Find out what is actually true about the NexaCare matter, from the data, before
you argue anything. Plan versus actual, task completion, how time was captured.

Then score **D × V × F > R** — dissatisfaction, vision, first steps, resistance.
It is a product, not a sum: if any term is zero, nothing happens.

Expect this to be uncomfortable. That is the point.

### 2. Guiding coalition & diffusion map · ~10 min

Who leads the change, and — harder — who do you approach first? Place the
personas on Rogers' adopter curve and test your coalition against Kotter's four
requirements: position power, expertise, credibility, leadership.

The intuitive answer is "start with the enthusiasts". It is usually wrong.

### 3. Change impact assessment · ~12 min · tabular

One row per persona, ten columns. Attach the **seven persona documents** as
rows.

Two columns do the real work: **what they lose**, and the **adoption signal**.
If your assessment says someone loses nothing, you have not looked hard enough.
People resist loss, not change.

### 4. Quick wins — 90 day plan · ~11 min

Three wins, each visible, measurable against a baseline you captured, and
deliverable inside 90 days. Kotter's distinction matters: short-term wins are
**created**, not hoped for.

Include the anti-victory line — what you will say when the first win lands, so
nobody declares the change finished.

### 5. Week 9 change pack · ~15 min

Assemble and export. Build the golden-thread table: every intervention traced
to a documented problem, every problem to an intervention or an explicit
decision not to act.

Export the **Process report**. For a change plan, showing how you reached a
position is usually more persuasive than the position.

## The question the week turns on

Every change plan can explain why someone *should* adopt it.

**Why would James Bentley actually do this?** Not should. Would.

A plan that can only answer the first question is the plan Kotter wrote his
article about.

## A note on the data

This is a teaching database and some figures are extreme by design. Say what
you are assuming — that the pattern is real even where the magnitude is
illustrative. A stated assumption earns marks; a hidden one loses them.
