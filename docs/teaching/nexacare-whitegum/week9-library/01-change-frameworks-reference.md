Index this document to the knowledge base. Several Week-9 workflows call

## Kotter — the eight errors

Kotter's 1995 article is usually taught as eight steps. It was written as eight
**errors**, and the errors are the more useful teaching object: the steps
describe what success looks like, the errors describe what people actually do.

| # | Error | The step it implies | The test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Not establishing a great enough sense of urgency | Establish urgency | Would roughly 75% of management genuinely agree the status quo is unacceptable? |
| 2 | Not creating a powerful enough guiding coalition | Form a coalition | Does it have position power, expertise, credibility and leadership, and is it led by a line leader? |
| 3 | Lacking a vision | Create a vision | Can it be conveyed in five minutes and produce understanding *and* interest? |
| 4 | Undercommunicating the vision by a factor of ten | Communicate the vision | Does it ride on existing routine forums, repeatedly, with visible walk-the-talk? |
| 5 | Not removing obstacles to the new vision | Empower others to act | Are the big obstacles confronted, or all of them routed around? |
| 6 | Not systematically planning for and creating short-term wins | Plan for short-term wins | Are wins engineered with baselines and dates, or merely hoped for? |
| 7 | Declaring victory too soon | Consolidate improvements | Does the plan run past the first win, and is there an explicit anti-victory rule? |
| 8 | Not anchoring changes in the corporation's culture | Institutionalise new approaches | Is there a route to "how we do things here" via results, promotion criteria and succession? |

Three of Kotter's own observations tend to be dropped in the retelling and are
worth keeping:

- **Urgency requires discomfort, sometimes manufactured.** Kotter's examples
  include deliberately surfacing bad news. His caveat is equally important:
  overdo it and you lose the people whose cooperation you need.
- **Renewal programmes fail without a leader who is a line manager.** A change
  driven from a staff function does not accumulate enough power.
- **Short-term wins are created, not hoped for.** The distinction is the
  difference between a plan and a wish.

## D × V × F > R (Beckhard–Harris)

Change happens when **Dissatisfaction** with the status quo, multiplied by a
**Vision** of what is possible, multiplied by **First** concrete steps, exceeds
the **Resistance**.

The form is multiplicative, and that is the whole point: if any term is zero,
the product is zero, and no amount of the other two rescues it. A superb vision
in a comfortable organisation is zero. Widespread frustration with no
articulated future is zero. Both plus no idea what to do on Monday is zero.

Most change programmes over-invest in V — it is the enjoyable term to work on —
and under-invest in D, because establishing dissatisfaction in a profitable
firm is politically uncomfortable.

## Rogers — diffusion of innovations

| Segment | Share | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| Innovators | 2.5% | Access and permission. They will find it themselves. |
| Early adopters | 13.5% | Credible early evidence. **These are the opinion leaders — they are who you design for.** |
| Early majority | 34% | Proof it works *here*, from someone like them. |
| Late majority | 34% | It to be the norm, and the old path to be closed. |
| Laggards | 16% | A requirement, or an exemption. |

The common error is to design the launch for the early majority, who are the
biggest group. They will not move on a launch; they move on evidence from
early adopters. The early-adopter cohort is therefore the design audience,
and the wins in the first 90 days exist largely to give them something to
report.

## Vroom — expectancy theory

Motivation = **Expectancy** × **Instrumentality** × **Valence**.

| Term | The question the person is asking | Where it usually fails in a law firm |
|---|---|---|
| Expectancy | If I put in effort, will I actually be able to do this? | Training absent; the productivity dip during learning is unacknowledged. |
| Instrumentality | If I do it well, will it lead to the outcome I was promised? | The reward system still measures billable hours. This is the term that most often reads zero. |
| Valence | Do I want that outcome? | The benefit accrues to the firm, or to the client, not to the person being asked to change. |

Multiplicative again. Communicating benefits harder does not fix an
Instrumentality of zero — only changing what gets measured does.

## Prosci — ADKAR and change impact assessment

**ADKAR** is the individual-level sequence: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge,
Ability, Reinforcement. Its use is diagnostic — when adoption stalls, identify
which letter is missing, because the intervention differs completely. Awareness
gaps need communication; Ability gaps need practice and time; Reinforcement
gaps need the measurement system to notice.

**Change impact assessment** asks, per affected group: what type of change is
this (process / technology / role / mindset), what is the current state, what
is the future state, how large is the impact on them, and what is the
transition strategy.

Two columns are added in this course, and they do most of the work:

- **What they lose.** People are resistant to loss, not to change. An
  assessment recording no loss for anyone has not been done properly — even a
  clearly beneficial change costs someone the expertise they had accumulated
  in the old way.
- **Adoption signal.** The observable behaviour that proves adoption, visible
  in data the firm already captures. A survey response is a claim; a change in
  the time ledger is evidence.

## Aphorisms used as tests in the Week-9 workflows

These come from the lecture and are deployed in the workflows as pass/fail
tests rather than decoration.

- *"If a change happens in the forest but nobody is around to measure it, did
  it make a difference?"* — every claimed improvement needs a baseline, a
  target and a date.
- *"Don't wait for perfect, you'll always be waiting."* — prefer the plan that
  could start on Monday.
- *"Tolerance for mistakes: for people (high); for technology (zero)."* — plans
  that assume flawless people, or flawless tooling, fail differently but both
  fail.

## Trust

Ipsos Australia's 2024 trustworthiness rankings place lawyers low, and the
relevant point for change work is internal rather than reputational: a message
is only as strong as its source is trusted **by that specific audience**.
Delivering a true message through a distrusted messenger is worse than
silence, because it consumes the opportunity to say it.

This is why the communication template pairs "delivered by" with "believed by
this audience because".
